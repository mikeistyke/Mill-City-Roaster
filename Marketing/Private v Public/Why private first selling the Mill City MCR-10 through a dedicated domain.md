# Why private first: selling the Mill City MCR-10 through a dedicated domain

A dedicated site at `millcity10kg.com` is a defensible first investment for this one-roaster sale because the stated development and advertising budget is only **$375**, the seller can control the evidence and offer, and a short campaign can test buyer interest before committing to a broker. That supports a private-first experiment, not a conclusion that private selling is already proven to produce a higher return or a faster sale. The strongest position is to target qualified operators, direct them to a credible product page, convert voluntary inquiries into a documented sales pipeline, and retain a broker as a conditional next step.

## Executive judgment

**Recommendation: private-first, evidence-led, and time-bounded, rather than private-only.** Use the domain as the central sales page for a 30-day campaign, with decision reviews on Days 10 and 20. This recommendation assumes no existing exclusive listing agreement, adequate seller availability, verified ownership and condition, and no urgent deadline that makes a short test too costly.

The central distinction is between a website and a distribution system. A product-specific domain is an address and a presentation surface; outreach, referrals, search advertising and permitted industry postings bring prospective buyers to it. CoffeeTec offers a different bundle: it says it supplies industry marketing, buyer handling, negotiation and logistics, funded by a spread above the seller’s agreed payout, not a standard percentage deducted from that payout ([CoffeeTec selling process](https://coffeetec.com/pages/sell-equipment)).

Consequently, the intellectually defensible argument is not “a $375 website saves a guaranteed 15% commission.” It is “a low-cost direct channel may avoid a buyer-facing price spread, preserve flexibility and generate useful first-party inquiries, provided the seller can replace enough of the broker’s matching and trust functions.” Whether that produces superior realized ROI remains an empirical question.

## The product and the evidence boundary

The supplied campaign PDF describes one 2025 Mill City Roasters MCR-10, factory-crated, never installed or fired, with an asking price of $32,000 and free LTL freight up to 1,500 miles under stated conditions. These are seller-supplied campaign inputs, not independently authenticated facts about the machine. The PDF also shows an opened crate panel, so “factory-wrapped, never installed or fired” is more defensible than “never opened.”

The manufacturer lists the legacy MCR-10’s production period as 2018–2025 and its support status as “Supported” ([Mill City retired models](https://millcityroasters.com/pages/retired)). Its current M-Series 10 kg page displays $47,000, making the campaign’s $32,000 ask arithmetically $15,000, or approximately 31.9%, below that displayed price ([Mill City M-Series 10 kg](https://millcityroasters.com/products/m-series-10-kilogram-coffee-roaster)). This is a price reference, not a claim of equivalent specifications, warranty, delivered cost or immediate availability.

Before publishing, verify the serial plate, year, fuel configuration, electrical requirements, included accessories, ownership documentation, warranty transferability and actual freight quote. A professional-looking website cannot substitute for these facts. In particular, do not promise manufacturer warranty coverage merely because the unit has never been operated.

Research checked public sources on November 4, 2026, with fresh fetches of the principal CoffeeTec terms and relevant product-price pages. This report distinguishes vendor statements, user-supplied inputs, anecdotal customer accounts and explicitly hypothetical calculations. No matched comparison of private-domain versus broker sales of equivalent MCR-10 units was found in the reviewed material.

## Why the private route can work

### The causal argument

Selling this roaster requires several conditions to align: a buyer must need the capacity, be able to pay, trust the seller and machine, be able to install it, and accept the transaction terms. The domain helps primarily with presenting evidence and making contact easy. Targeted distribution helps the right buyer encounter that evidence; neither function alone closes the sale.

The proposed strategy is therefore a sequence of testable mechanisms, not an assertion that newer technology beats brokerage:

- **Target market:** Prioritize established 3–6 kg operators with demonstrable production constraints, wholesale demand or expansion plans. Treat this as a buyer-fit hypothesis from the campaign, not a measured census of likely purchasers.
- **Targeted distribution:** Place the offer in front of decision-makers already considering production equipment. Favor specific equipment searches, individual roastery outreach and relevant industry communities over broad audiences interested in drinking coffee.
- **Product-specific evidence:** Present the exact model, condition, serial documentation, included items, freight terms and inspection process in one place. The proposed advantage is lower buyer uncertainty, not the aesthetic value of React.
- **Direct negotiation:** Allow the buyer’s installation, payment and logistics questions to reach the owner without an intermediary. This is valuable only if the seller responds accurately and promptly.
- **Measured learning:** Record which sources produce qualified conversations and what objections stop progress. Use that information to revise the offer, rather than interpreting every page view as demand.

### Why the domain itself is not the advantage

The domain is memorable and descriptive, but it should not be treated as a shortcut to search ranking. Google explicitly says its exact-match-domain system avoids giving excessive ranking credit to domains designed to match particular queries ([Google ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide/)). The commercial case must therefore work through deliberate distribution even if organic search contributes little during the test.

React, Vite and Tailwind are implementation choices, not demonstrated drivers of buyer conversion. Within the ten-hour budget, prioritize a fast mobile page, usable photographs, legible specifications, a visible price, clear freight conditions and a tested inquiry route. Avoid a custom checkout, chatbot, account system or paid enrichment integration unless an observed buyer need justifies it.

The page should clearly identify itself as an independent private sale, not an official Mill City website or authorized dealership. Use the brand to identify the actual product, not to imply endorsement. The strongest trust signal is verifiable ownership and transaction transparency, not a manufacturer-like visual identity.

### CoffeeTec’s fee structure changes the comparison

CoffeeTec’s published terms say the seller receives the amount agreed before listing, while CoffeeTec retains the difference between that amount and the final selling price ([listing agreement](https://coffeetec.com/pages/coffeetec-listing-terms-and-conditions)). ### Break-even conditions

Let \(M\) be an avoided broker spread, \(C\) the private campaign cost, and \(\Delta K\) the private route’s additional seller labor and closing costs relative to brokerage. At an equal buyer budget and conditional on a completed sale, private selling wins when:

\[
M > C + \Delta K
\]

With only the known $375 included, the threshold is a $375 spread. However, brokerage might support a higher buyer willingness to pay through trust, convenience or financing access; in that case, the entire observed spread is not necessarily recoverable by a private seller. CoffeeTec advertises financing and transaction support, but the dollar value of those services for this buyer and machine is unmeasured ([CoffeeTec FAQ](https://coffeetec.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions)).

If both routes have the same probability \(q\) of selling within the evaluation period, identical unsold-asset value and otherwise identical economics, the expected private advantage becomes:

\[
\Delta E = qM - C
\]

For a hypothetical $1,500 spread and $375 campaign cost, break-even is a 25% sale probability under those assumptions. This is not a forecast that the campaign has a 25% chance of success. It demonstrates why “low setup cost” is insufficient without some credible prospect of a sale.

### A rigorous decision model includes the unsold asset

The more general measure is expected seller value over a fixed horizon, such as 30 days, rather than gross revenue divided by advertising spend. For channel \(j\), define:

\[
E_j = q_j N_j + (1-q_j)V_j - C_j - H_j - L_j
\]

- **\(q_j\):** Probability of completing the sale by the horizon, currently unknown.
- **\(N_j\):** Seller proceeds if sold, after channel spread, freight and closing costs; use expected negotiated proceeds, not the advertised ask.
- **\(V_j\):** Retained value of the unsold roaster at the horizon, net of future disposal obligations not counted elsewhere.
- **\(C_j\):** Campaign spending incurred whether or not the sale succeeds.
- **\(H_j\):** Expected seller labor, storage and time-related costs over the period.
- **\(L_j\):** Expected transaction losses not already reflected in proceeds or retained value.

Compare \(E_{\text{private}}\) with \(E_{\text{broker}}\), using the same horizon and consistent cost allocation. The unsold machine is not worth zero merely because it did not sell within thirty days. Likewise, the historical purchase price matters for total asset profit but should not be deducted from one channel and omitted from the other.

Dividing $30,000 by $375 produces an 80-to-1 gross proceeds-to-campaign-cost ratio, not an 8,000% marketing ROI. It ignores the machine’s value, sale probability, negotiated price, labor, freight and the possibility the same buyer would have arrived without the advertisement. A valid incremental ROI needs a credible counterfactual, which this one-unit campaign does not yet provide.

## Contract flexibility is a real economic difference

CoffeeTec’s published equipment agreement is an exclusive, 30-day revolving listing arrangement, requires termination notice by email rather than text, and directs seller-received inquiries to CoffeeTec unless a written exception is agreed ([listing agreement](https://coffeetec.com/pages/coffeetec-listing-terms-and-conditions)). It also states that CoffeeTec is entitled to 15% of the website listing price under specified circumstances involving a buyer it acquires and a seller’s refusal to sell or direct sale without the required notice ([listing agreement](https://coffeetec.com/pages/coffeetec-listing-terms-and-conditions)).

**That 15% is not the routine sales commission.** It is a contractual consequence described in the agreement, and its application should be clarified before acceptance ([listing agreement](https://coffeetec.com/pages/coffeetec-listing-terms-and-conditions)). At a hypothetical $32,000 website listing price, 15% would be $4,800, a material exposure relative to the $375 test budget.

The agreement also describes a $500 limit on CoffeeTec liability, broad seller indemnification, and a seller representation that the equipment is in full working order unless otherwise noted in writing ([listing agreement](https://coffeetec.com/pages/coffeetec-listing-terms-and-conditions)). For an uninstalled, unfired machine, document precisely what has and has not been tested rather than allowing an unqualified operational representation. This is commercial issue-spotting, not a legal opinion on enforceability; obtain qualified review before accepting consequential terms.

- **Before any submission:** Check whether an agreement already exists. A request for information should not inadvertently become acceptance of an exclusive listing.
- **If selling privately first:** Obtain a nonbinding broker quote without accepting a listing agreement; preserve the option to engage later.
- **If combining channels:** Obtain a written carve-out for the domain and pre-existing or self-generated leads, including pricing and fee treatment.
- **If already exclusive:** Follow the agreement’s routing and termination provisions. Do not assume the private site may transact independently.
- **If moving to the broker:** Agree in writing on seller proceeds, displayed price, freight allocation, payment milestone and any continuing role for the domain.

## What $125 of advertising can and cannot establish

The ad budget should test a targeting hypothesis, not be described as enough to “identify the market” conclusively. Google’s Keyword Planner provides search and cost estimates, but Google notes that campaign results depend on bid, budget, ad quality, location, product and customer behavior ([Keyword Planner guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en)). No account-level forecast or observed CPC for this campaign was available for this report.

The following is deliberately hypothetical. Let \(n=\lfloor125/\text{CPC}\rfloor\), and let \(r\) be the probability that one relevant click becomes a qualified inquiry; under an independent, equal-probability model, the chance of at least one qualified inquiry is \(1-(1-r)^n\).

| Illustrative case | Assumed CPC | Clicks within $125 | Assumed qualified-inquiry rate | Expected qualified inquiries | Chance of at least one qualified inquiry |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cautious | $10 | 12 | 1% | 0.12 | 11.4% |
| Middle sensitivity | $5 | 25 | 3% | 0.75 | 53.3% |
| Favorable | $2 | 62 | 5% | 3.10 | 95.8% |

These are not industry benchmarks, confidence intervals or sale probabilities. Repeat visitors, heterogeneous intent and dependent clicks weaken the simple model. The table’s purpose is to show how easily a small budget can produce no qualified lead even when the channel is not intrinsically ineffective.

For example, zero qualified inquiries from 25 independent comparable visits leaves an exact one-sided 95% upper bound of approximately 11.3% on the underlying inquiry rate, calculated as \(1-0.05^{1/25}\). That wide uncertainty is a reason to inspect traffic quality and buyer objections, not to pronounce the domain a success or failure. A sale from one buyer likewise cannot prove that this channel would outperform brokerage across a population of machines.

### Recommended use of the test

- **Audience:** Established operators with demonstrable demand, appropriate space and a plausible equipment budget. A café group or funded startup is secondary unless its installation and funding readiness are clear.
- **Search intent:** Test terms such as “Mill City 10kg roaster for sale,” “MCR-10 for sale” and “used 10 kg coffee roaster.” Avoid relying on broad “coffee” or “Mill City” traffic without inspecting relevance.
- **Budget discipline:** Keep the $125 concentrated in one high-intent test rather than fragmenting it across many platforms. Use actual forecasts and observed query quality to decide whether paid search is viable.
- **Organic complement:** Send individually relevant outreach to the campaign’s proposed forty roasteries and use permitted industry posts or referrals. Log the seller time; “no media fee” does not mean no cost.
- **Offer:** Lead with documented condition, production fit and delivered-price clarity. Do not promise energy savings, warranty coverage, a specific resale value or installation readiness without evidence.
- **Primary action:** Request an inspection, documentation or delivered quote. Treat the domain as the place where the sale originates and is documented, not necessarily where a $32,000 card checkout occurs.

## Page hits are signals, not identified buyers

Cloudflare’s analytics provides page-view and visitor information while explicitly stating that it does not collect or use visitors’ personal data ([Cloudflare Web Analytics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/about/)). Consequently, “gathering page hits to identify potential leads” should mean identifying promising traffic sources and encouraging voluntary inquiries, not obtaining the names and emails of anonymous visitors.

Use a short inquiry form or direct contact option to convert anonymous interest into a known conversation. Capture name, business, email or phone, location, buying timeframe and the buyer’s question; collect further budget and installation details during qualification. Explain how information will be used, minimize collection, and do not repurpose a sales inquiry into unrelated marketing without an appropriate basis.

| Measurement stage | Record | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | Ad impressions or outreach delivered | The offer may have reached an audience |
| Visit | Page views, visits and available referrer data | Traffic, not identity or purchase intent |
| Inquiry | Successfully received form, email or call | A contactable person has expressed interest |
| Qualified opportunity | Decision-maker, plausible budget, site fit, timing and installation feasibility | A potentially viable purchaser |
| Transaction progress | Inspection, written offer, agreed terms, deposit status | Increasing commitment, not yet a completed sale |
| Completed sale | Cleared required funds, executed documentation and agreed handoff | The primary commercial result |

Use campaign-tagged links and explicitly save the source with inquiry records if the chosen form implementation supports it. Do not assume that enabling basic page analytics automatically provides custom conversion tracking or a CRM. Test a complete inquiry from a phone, verify receipt, and maintain a simple source-and-status ledger.

For each channel, calculate cost per qualified opportunity using both advertising-only cost and an all-in cost that includes allocated labor. Record first reported discovery and subsequent touches where known, because a buyer may find the machine in an industry post and return through the domain. Attribute conservatively; the final click is not necessarily the cause of the sale.

## A bounded execution and decision plan

The supplied PDF’s thirty-day objective is useful as a management horizon, not a promised close date. The following checkpoints are proposed operating rules rather than statistically validated thresholds. If funds are needed immediately, request the broker comparison now instead of spending most of the available time on an unproven channel.

| Period | Action | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–3 | Verify identity, condition and freight; build page and contact route; check any existing listing restrictions | Do not launch with unresolved ownership, material specification or freight claims |
| Days 4–10 | Start targeted outreach and the concentrated ad test; record sources and objections | If traffic is irrelevant, repair targeting; if inquiries fail to arrive technically, repair the contact path |
| Day 10 review | Review qualified conversations, not raw visits | Continue if there is a credible inspection or materially advancing buyer; zero qualified conversations triggers diagnosis and a broker quote review |
| Days 11–20 | Follow up, address common objections, provide inspections and precise delivered quotes | Do not increase spend just to generate more page hits |
| Day 20 review | Compare live opportunities with broker payout, buyer-facing price, terms and likely timing | If no credible buyer is progressing, consider the broker rather than defending the original channel choice |
| Days 21–30 | Advance a live offer or make the channel transition deliberately | Stop, extend or switch based on evidence and carrying cost; do not silently exceed the budget |

Ten programming hours should buy a tightly scoped sales tool, not an elaborate application. A reasonable planning allocation is two hours for structure and assets, three for responsive layout, two for product evidence and contact handling, one for analytics/source capture, and two for deployment and testing. This is a proposed allocation of the user’s estimate, not an independent contractor quote or a guarantee that every integration fits.

The missing quantities most likely to change the decision are the actual CoffeeTec spread and seller payout, seller sales hours, freight cost, urgency, qualified-buyer response and any existing exclusivity. Obtain those before treating the model’s sensitivity examples as realized savings. Do not let the purchased domain become a reason to continue an ineffective campaign indefinitely.

## Final position: why private first is the most defensible starting point

The private-first case rests on bounded cost, control and the opportunity to learn, not on hostility to brokers. At the stated budget, the seller can test a coherent proposition: a precisely documented, never-operated MCR-10 offered directly to operators for whom its capacity solves a real production problem. If that proposition produces qualified conversations, the domain can support negotiations without an additional broker spread; if it does not, the seller has learned where the obstacles lie before committing further.

This approach is reliable as a decision process because it names the target, makes the offer inspectable, measures movement toward a transaction and specifies when to change course. It is not proven to be the most reliable closing channel, because comparative sale probabilities, net prices and elapsed times remain unknown. CoffeeTec becomes the better choice when its incremental buyer access, trust, financing support, saved seller effort or faster completion is worth more than its effective price spread and contractual constraints.

The recommended position is therefore: **own the presentation, target the market, measure qualified demand, and earn the right to continue selling privately.** Keep brokerage available on clarified terms rather than assuming either channel is inherently superior. The website is justified as a modest, controlled sales experiment; superior ROI must be demonstrated by buyer progress and net proceeds, not inferred from the existence of a domain.
