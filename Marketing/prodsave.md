# 2025 Mill City Roasters MCR-10: Problems It Solves and Where It Saves Money

A buyer-facing value story for one new-in-crate, never-fired 2025 Mill City Roasters MCR-10 (10 kg, gas or propane), asking $32,000 with free LTL freight up to 1,500 miles.

The short version: this machine does about twice the work per labor hour of a 5 kg roaster. Labor is where the big savings come from. Energy savings are real but smaller. The other gains are more output, fewer roast days, and less wear per pound.

## Problems This Roaster Solves

### Production and capacity
- **A small roaster can't keep up.** When volume grows, a 5 kg roaster gets stretched thin. Per [CoffeeTec's sizing guide](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/what-size-coffee-roaster-do-you-need), a 5 kg machine takes about 24 hours for a workload that a 10–12 kg machine does in about 10. The same guide says "one new account pushes it over the line."
- **Saying no to wholesale accounts.** Extra capacity means a new café or grocery account doesn't force night or weekend shifts.
- **Long roast days.** Twice the beans per batch means half as many batches for the same pounds.
- **Wanting to grow but not over-buy.** The 2 kg minimum batch still handles samples, single-origin lots, and small custom orders.
- **Waiting for a new machine.** The new M-Series 10 kg is listed as "call for availability" ([Mill City](https://millcityroasters.com/products/m-series-10-kilogram-coffee-roaster)). This unit is crated and ready to ship.

### Labor and payroll
- **Payroll scales with how many hours the roaster runs.** A traditional drum roaster needs someone watching the whole roast ([Bellwether](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/commercial-coffee-roaster-buying-guide)). When each batch is twice as big, the operator spends about half as many hours per pound.
- **Overtime.** Production roasters are usually hourly, overtime-eligible employees ([ZipRecruiter listing, Groundwork](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Production-Roaster)). Moving 59 roasting hours a week down to about 30 can take a second shift or overtime off the schedule.
- **More coffee per labor hour.** CoffeeTec says one larger roaster gives "more coffee per labor hour, with a single machine to maintain" ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/what-size-coffee-roaster-do-you-need)).
- **Reliance on one skilled roaster.** RoastPATH Roast Replay can repeat saved profiles. A trained helper can run approved profiles, so the head roaster doesn't have to babysit every batch.

### Energy and utilities
- **Partly empty batches waste fuel.** Heating the drum and moving air takes about the same energy whether the batch is full or not. Small or half-full batches can use two to three times more energy per pound ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/commercial-electric-coffee-roaster)).
- **Larger roasters use less energy per pound.** Full batches need about 570–1,400 BTU per pound of green coffee, and larger roasters land toward the low end ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/commercial-electric-coffee-roaster)).
- **Warm-up time is wasted energy.** Every roast day starts with a warm-up. Fewer roast days means fewer warm-ups.
- **Gas is cheaper to run and to install.** Diedrich's roast-summit analysis says electric roasters cost more to run and set up than gas ones ([Diedrich](https://www.diedrichroasters.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Karl-Roast-Summit-the-True-Costs-of-Electricity-Slides-compressed.pdf)). Gas prices are steady right now: EIA projects Henry Hub at $3.48/MMBtu for 2026 and $3.16 for 2027 ([EIA STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/natgas.php?mod=article_inline)).
- **Works with or without a gas line.** It runs on natural gas or propane, so a site without a gas line can still use it.
- **No costly electrical upgrade.** A 5 kg electric roaster needs about 16 kW and "almost always" three-phase power ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/commercial-electric-coffee-roaster)). The MCR-10 runs on a 20A, 220V single-phase circuit.

### Maintenance and wear parts
- **Wear parts are cheap, stocked consumables.** Thermocouples cost $35–$70 and cooling-tray Teflon wear strips start at $24. Food-grade bearing grease and chain lubricant are sold off the shelf ([Mill City parts](https://millcityroasters.com/collections/coffee-roaster-parts-accessories), [Mill City tech support](https://millcityroasters.com/pages/coffee-roaster-tech-support)).
- **Long shutdowns waiting on parts.** Mill City stocks over 8,000 replacement parts in Minneapolis and can ship overnight in the U.S. ([Mill City](https://millcityroasters.com/pages/coffee-roaster-tech-support)). It still supports the retired MCR-10 with venting kits, gas orifices, regulators, lubricants, and thermocouples ([Mill City retired models](https://millcityroasters.com/pages/retired)).
- **Less wear per pound.** The drum, motor, chain, and bearings wear based on how many hours they run. Half the run hours for the same output means about half the wear per pound. Bearings and chains should last longer before they need replacing. (This is reasoning from run hours, not a manufacturer figure.)
- **Unknown wear on used machines.** Used MCR-10s sell for $19,900–$29,500 ([Coffee Equipment Pros](https://coffeeequipmentpros.com/collections/mill-city-roasters)) and come with roast hours already on them. This unit has zero.
- **Cleaning time.** Quick-Connect venting kits ($350) let you take the exhaust apart quickly and clean it often ([Mill City](https://millcityroasters.com/products/quick-connect-kits-for-mcr-10)).

### Margin and supply
- **Paying for roasted coffee.** Café owners who switched to roasting in-house report going from $9–$11/lb for roasted coffee to about $4–$5/lb ([Bellwether](https://bellwethercoffee.com/blog/coffee-roasting-profit-margins)). A 10 kg machine brings that margin to multi-unit and wholesale volumes.
- **No control over freshness, blends, or private label.** Roasting your own coffee gives you control of the profile, schedule, and branding.

## Payroll Story: Same Coffee, Half the Hours

Example: a roastery producing **1,000 lb of roasted coffee per week**.

To be conservative, the comparison uses CoffeeTec's real-world planning rate, not the 66 lb/hr nameplate rate. That rate is about 7.7 kg (17 lb) roasted per hour on a 5 kg machine ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/what-size-coffee-roaster-do-you-need)). The example assumes about 34 lb/hr on the 10 kg MCR-10, or double the 5 kg rate. Wages use the U.S. average of $20.40/hr for coffee roasters ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Coffee-Roaster)).

| Metric | 5 kg roaster | MCR-10 (10 kg) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roasted lb per roasting hour (planning rate) | ~17 | ~34 | 2x |
| Roasting hours per week | ~59 | ~29.5 | −29.5 hrs |
| Weekly roasting wages at $20.40/hr | ~$1,204 | ~$602 | −$602/week |
| Annual roasting wages (52 weeks) | ~$62,600 | ~$31,300 | **~$31,300/year** |
| Overtime exposure | ~19 hrs over 40 if one roaster runs everything | None | Overtime gone |

**What this shows:** one roaster on a standard 40-hour week can produce this volume with time to spare. The 5 kg shop needs either overtime or a second person. If the 5 kg shop pays overtime at time-and-a-half, the gap grows by another ~$190/week. At about $31,000 a year in labor savings, the $32,000 price pays for itself in about a year from payroll alone. Install costs are separate (see below).

If you don't need to cut hours, the same math works in reverse. Keep the same labor hours and roast about twice the coffee, without hiring.

## Energy Story: Full Batches, Fewer Warm-Ups

The MCR-10 doesn't win on burner size. It wins by **running full batches and fewer hours**.

**Assumptions** (example only; use your own bills):
- 1,000 lb roasted per week is about 1,200 lb of green coffee. That's 15–20% weight loss during roasting ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/best-coffee-roaster-machines-for-small-business)).
- A 5 kg machine running near its limit, often on partial or rushed batches: ~1,200 BTU per lb of green. A 10 kg machine on full batches: ~800 BTU per lb. Both are within CoffeeTec's 570–1,400 BTU range ([CoffeeTec](https://coffeetec.com/blogs/guide/commercial-electric-coffee-roaster)).
- Virginia commercial natural gas averaged $11.18 per Mcf (thousand cubic feet) in 2025 ([Commercial Energy Advisors, EIA data](https://commercialenergyadvisors.com/resources/commercial-natural-gas-rates-by-state-2026/)). That's about $10.80 per MMBtu.
- Fan and motor electricity: ~2.5 kW average and $0.13/kWh. **Both numbers are assumptions; replace them with your own utility rate.**

| Energy line | 5 kg roaster | MCR-10 | Annual savings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roasting gas (MMBtu/yr) | ~75 | ~50 | ~25 MMBtu, about **$270** |
| Warm-ups (roast days/week) | ~7.4 | ~3.7 | ~5 MMBtu, about **$55** |
| Fan/motor run hours per year | ~3,070 | ~1,535 | ~3,800 kWh, about **$500** |
| **Total energy** | | | **about $800–$850/year** |

**What this shows:** energy savings are steady but small next to labor, roughly 2–3% of the payroll savings. The selling point is that every pound costs less to roast. Fewer burner hours, warm-ups, and motor hours also mean less heat in the building and less exhaust running. Gas prices in your state will change the dollar amount.

## Costs a Buyer Should Plan For

To be credible, the pitch should include the costs:
- **Installation:** a 1/2" NPT gas line, a dedicated 220V circuit, and venting, all done by licensed tradespeople ([MCR-10 manual](https://manuals.plus/m/13466d9e62b7b565ee2bb5b5b6520e25e7332db0d27ad6fe7f0c4fd3066c908d.pdf)). Get local quotes.
- **Space:** about 68 x 39 x 76 in and about 904 lb. The assembled Layout A footprint is about 5'0" x 8'10" ([Mill City](https://millcityroasters.com/products/quick-connect-kits-for-mcr-10)).
- **Warranty:** check coverage with Mill City using the serial number. Paid tech support is $150–$200/hr ([Mill City](https://millcityroasters.com/pages/coffee-roaster-tech-support)).
- **It only pays off if you use the capacity.** A 10 kg machine running half-empty batches loses its energy advantage. It fits buyers already running back-to-back batches on a 3–6 kg roaster.
