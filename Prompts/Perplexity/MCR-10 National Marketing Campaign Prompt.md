# Prompt: National Marketing Campaign for a New-in-Box 2025 Mill City Roasters MCR-10 (10 kg) Coffee Roaster

---

## INSTRUCTION

You are a senior B2B equipment marketer and copywriter who specializes in commercial coffee and food-service equipment. Build a complete, ready-to-launch national (U.S.) marketing campaign to sell one never-opened, never-used 2025 Mill City Roasters MCR-10 10-kilogram commercial gas coffee roaster at an asking price of $32,000 USD.

Structure the campaign around the 5W1H framework:

- **WHO** buys a 10 kg roaster: define 3–5 buyer personas with their pain points, triggers, objections, and where they spend time online and offline.
- **WHAT** is being sold: an accurate, spec-driven product story that makes clear this is the 2025 MCR-10 (legacy model), not the new 2026 M-Series, and explains why that is still a strong value.
- **WHEN** to launch and how to pace it: a 30-day campaign calendar with urgency cues (one unit only, factory-sealed, priced below new).
- **WHERE** to list and promote it: ranked marketplaces, communities, and channels where commercial roaster buyers actually look.
- **WHY** a buyer should choose this unit over a new M-Series or a used MCR-10: a clear value case based on price, condition, lead time, and proven performance.
- **HOW** the sale and delivery work: pricing and negotiation strategy, freight and shipping terms, payment safety, inspection, and handoff.

Rules:

1. Be accurate. Do not call this unit an "M-Series" or claim M-Series features (such as the Minneapolis monolithic chassis, haptic jog wheel, or remote diagnostics) unless the seller confirms them from the serial plate.
2. Do not promise a manufacturer warranty. Mill City's original warranty does not extend beyond the original purchaser, so present it as "warranty status: confirm with Mill City using the serial number" and offer that buyers can buy hourly tech support from Mill City.
3. Write for commercial buyers. Use plain, confident, professional language with no hype, filler, or emojis.
4. Every price comparison must cite a real, current source with a URL.
