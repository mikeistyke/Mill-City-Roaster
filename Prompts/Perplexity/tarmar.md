# Target-market research prompt: Mill City MCR-10

## Instruction

Act as a U.S. commercial coffee-roasting equipment market researcher and B2B sales analyst. Identify and rank the most likely buyers for the specific roaster described below. Research the market and documented purchases; do not merely rewrite a sales advertisement or repeat the attached campaign's assumptions.

Organize the analysis around seven purchase drivers: **age replacement, capacity upgrade, brand allegiance, industry economics, coffee consumption, U.S. tariffs, and energy considerations**. Explain how each driver affects demand for this particular unit, which businesses it favors, and which businesses it disqualifies. Identify both positive and negative evidence.

Use current, verifiable information as of the date you conduct the research. State that date. Prefer manufacturer documentation, public business announcements, equipment invoices or transaction records voluntarily published, auction results, dealer sold archives, government data, utilities, and credible industry research. Cite factual claims inline with source links and publication or transaction dates when available. Separate seller-provided statements, externally verified facts, analytical estimates, and unknowns.

### Nonnegotiable geography

- Origin: **Harrisonburg, Virginia, ZIP code 22801**. Treat 22801 as a ZIP code, not a telephone area code.
- Eligible prospects must have their actual delivery/installation address **no farther than 1,500 one-way driving/road miles from the origin**. Do not substitute straight-line distance, a state boundary, a metro centroid, or a round-trip distance.
- Both destination and entire trucking route must remain inside the contiguous United States. Exclude Canada, Mexico, international destinations, and routes that cross an international border even if both endpoints are in the United States. Exclude Alaska, Hawaii, and territories requiring noncontiguous transport.
- Determine mileage using a documented mapping/routing source, with a U.S.-only truck-feasible route where available. Record origin precision, destination precision, route source, distance, and verification date. If only ZIP-to-ZIP routing is available, label it preliminary and require address-level confirmation before freight is promised.
- Do not assume an entire state or metro is eligible. Assess each delivery location. Put unverified or boundary-sensitive locations in a separate validation queue, not in the qualified prospect list.
- The geographic limit is absolute. Do not recommend expanding it to close a sale. These instructions supersede any radius-extension suggestions in the attached campaign.

### Freight and delivered-price economics

Use the following as **seller-provided planning assumptions**, not independently established industry facts:

- Working advertised selling price: **$32,000**, taken from the attached campaign, unless the seller supplies a revised price.
- Delivery is included in that selling price, with an allowance of **up to $4,000**. Do not add ordinary included delivery to the advertised price again.
- Seller's modeled shipping rate: **$2.00 per one-way road mile**.
- User-provided comparison rate: **$2.30 per one-way road mile**. Verify whether this is a reasonable current benchmark before calling it an industry-wide rate. Do not confuse full-truckload per-mile averages with an actual LTL quote for this shipment.

For each qualified prospect, calculate:

- Modeled line-haul freight = road miles × $2.00.
- Comparison line-haul freight = road miles × $2.30.
- Modeled freight advantage = road miles × $0.30.
- Remaining freight allowance = $4,000 minus modeled line-haul freight minus separately identified accessorials/insurance, if known.
- Seller proceeds after freight = delivered selling price minus modeled line-haul freight minus known accessorials/insurance. Label this **proceeds after freight, not profit**; acquisition cost, selling fees, taxes, and other costs remain separate.

Reconcile the allowance explicitly: at 1,500 miles, modeled line-haul is **$3,000**, the comparison is **$3,450**, and the modeled advantage is **$450**. The remaining **$1,000** of the allowance is a possible reserve, not proof that accessorials are covered and not permission to expand the radius to 2,000 miles. Do not automatically spend or deduct the entire $4,000 for every delivery.

Validate actual freight with current shipment-specific quotes where obtainable. Separate crating, loading, liftgate, limited-access/residential delivery, insurance, fuel surcharges, and unloading requirements; avoid double-counting items already included in a quote. Flag unknowns and determine whether any quoted delivery exceeds the $4,000 allowance. Do not invent buyer-paid excess charges, guaranteed coverage, or a shipping policy not authorized by the seller.

### Required research workstreams

**1. Age replacement**

Identify businesses with older, heavily used, maintenance-intensive, unsupported, or downtime-prone commercial roasters near this machine's practical capacity. Research equipment model, approximate commissioning/purchase date, service history, support availability, and replacement signals when public. Machine age alone is not evidence of failure or purchase intent; do not invent a standard replacement life. Distinguish same-capacity replacement from an actual capacity upgrade.

**2. Capacity upgrade**

Prioritize roasteries moving from approximately 3–6 kg batches, but test rather than assume their suitability. Seek evidence of wholesale growth, new accounts, additional cafés, production hiring, extended roasting hours, facility expansion, or a stated equipment search. Estimate practical capacity only from documented batch times, loading, cooling, roast loss, staffing, and utilization assumptions. Separate green-coffee input from roasted-coffee output. Show when 10 kg would be too large, too small, or operationally incompatible.

**3. Brand allegiance**

Identify existing Mill City users, publicly documented prior Mill City buyers, and businesses whose staff or workflows are demonstrably familiar with the brand. Assess switching costs, software compatibility, training, support, parts availability, and willingness to buy a retired model from a private seller. Do not infer brand loyalty from follows, likes, or a single unverified photograph. Distinguish a current owner from a historical buyer and from a buyer of this exact model. Verify warranty transferability and legacy-model service support rather than promising them.

**4. Industry economics**

Analyze small-roastery and multi-location café economics: green-coffee costs, labor, financing, lease/build-out requirements, installation, wholesale margins, outsourcing versus in-house roasting, and capital budgets. Compare this unit's delivered and installed cost with genuinely comparable new and used alternatives, documenting what each price includes. Use labeled scenarios for payback or break-even calculations; do not invent a prospect's sales, cash balance, profit, or financing eligibility. Consider whether high input costs deter investment as well as encourage efficiency upgrades.

**5. Coffee consumption**

Research current U.S. and relevant regional coffee consumption, specialty demand, retail/wholesale trends, local business growth, and observable demand signals. Prefer sources such as the National Coffee Association, USDA, Census, BLS, and credible local economic sources. Distinguish consumer survey findings from sales volumes and dollars from physical consumption. Do not infer a company's purchasing intent or local roaster demand solely from national per-capita consumption. Explain the connection between consumption evidence and a realistic need for this production scale.

**6. U.S. tariffs**

Check tariffs and trade rules effective on the research date using CBP, USITC/HTS, USTR, Federal Register notices, and other authoritative sources. Distinguish tariffs on imported finished roasting equipment or components from tariffs on green or roasted coffee. Verify relevant country of origin and classification where possible; do not guess an HTS code or a tariff rate. Mark announced, proposed, stayed, exempted, and effective measures distinctly.

Determine whether the unit is already domestically owned and customs-cleared. Do not automatically apply a new import duty to a domestic resale. Evaluate tariffs' possible effect on replacement-equipment prices and buyer budgets without claiming this unit has a confirmed tariff advantage when origin, import status, or classification remains unknown. Explain both potential demand benefits and countervailing pressure on buyers' coffee-input costs.

**7. Energy considerations**

Assess the unit's verified fuel configuration, gas/propane availability, electrical requirements, ventilation/emissions needs, local installation restrictions, and applicable permitting. Compare regional commercial natural-gas, propane, and electricity costs using dated EIA, utility, or supplier data. Distinguish burner rating from actual consumption: do not treat a 90,000 BTU/hour input rating as measured continuous consumption or electricity use. Where supported, model energy cost per batch or roasted pound using explicit utilization and roast-loss assumptions. Avoid unsupported claims that gas, electric, domestic, or newer machines are inherently more efficient. Include conversion feasibility, commissioning, and energy-related retrofit costs where relevant.

### Previous-purchase investigation

Compile a defensible list of **previous purchases of the exact product and comparable commercial roasters, both new and used**. Search exact Mill City MCR-10 transactions first; then expand to other Mill City commercial models and approximately 6–15 kg commercial production roasters from other brands when necessary. Explain comparability in capacity, age, condition, fuel, features, support, and transaction timing. Do not use sample/home roasters as primary comparables.

Classify every record as one of the following:

- **Confirmed purchase:** a buyer announcement, manufacturer case study, invoice, auction result, or other explicit evidence of a completed purchase.
- **Dealer-marked sold:** a listing explicitly marked sold, but buyer identity, final price, or completion details may remain undisclosed.
- **Owner/install evidence only:** evidence that a business operates a machine, without proof of a purchase event or transaction price.
- **Active/archived asking price only:** an offer or historical advertisement, not evidence of a completed sale.

Keep confirmed purchases and dealer-marked sold records in the historical-transactions table, with their different confidence levels visible. Place owner/install signals and asking-price listings in separate tables. A sold listing's displayed price is not automatically the final transaction price. Preserve distinctions among listing date, model year, sale date, and installation date. De-duplicate relisted machines using serial numbers, photos, seller, model year, location, and dates where available.

For each transaction, record brand/model, batch capacity, model year if known, condition, buyer or seller when public, installation/delivery location, evidence classification, transaction date, confirmed final price if public, displayed asking price separately, currency, included accessories/freight/tax if known, source URL, and verification notes. Write **not disclosed** rather than filling gaps with guesses.

The primary purchase-history list must concern U.S. buyers/destinations within the eligible 1,500-road-mile area. A machine offered by an in-area seller does not prove an eligible historical buyer. If important price comparables lack a known qualifying destination or fall outside the geography, place them only in a clearly labeled **out-of-scope/unknown-location price-reference appendix**; never count them as target-market purchases or eligible leads. Online sources may be anywhere, but delivery eligibility cannot be waived.

A past purchase is a market signal, not proof of current demand. Recent buyers may have low near-term replacement interest. Explain whether each historical purchaser is a prospective buyer, a brand-affinity signal, or only a pricing comparable.

### Evidence and prioritization

Research toward **25 qualified business prospects and 15 historical transaction records**, prioritizing evidence quality over counts. If fewer can be verified, return the verified subset, report the shortfall, and provide a research queue rather than fabricated rows. Separate confirmed fit from inferred fit. Use public business contact channels only; do not seek private personal information.

Score eligible prospects with a transparent, nonprobabilistic 100-point prioritization rubric:

|---|---:|
| Verified replacement or capacity-upgrade trigger | 30 |

Treat unknowns as unknowns, not automatic negatives or invented positives. The score is a research prioritization tool, not a calibrated purchase probability. Geographic failure is a disqualification, not a point deduction. Discuss coffee consumption, tariffs, and energy as evidenced modifiers of fit and economics rather than speculative extra points.
